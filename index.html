Atlas HR Analytics

Why are employees leaving, and who is most likely to leave next?

Overview
  • Demographics
  • Performance Tracker
  • Attrition

I built a four-page Power BI report that gives HR one place to track headcount, the make-up of the workforce and who is leaving. Its Performance Tracker lets a manager pick any employee and see their satisfaction scores and review ratings year by year, ready for the next review.

Built with Power BI, Power Query, DAX and data modeling.

View on GitHub (opens in a new tab)

What it found

  • Overtime is the clearest warning sign: staff who work overtime leave at 30.5%, nearly three times the 10.4% of those who don’t.
  • The first two years carry the most risk. Attrition is over 30% for people with less than two years at the company, and around 5% or less after eight.
  • Sales Representatives and Recruiters have the highest attrition of any role, at roughly 40% and 37%.

Sales Data Analysis

Where does an online retailer’s revenue really come from, and where should it focus next?

Revenue momentum
  • Markets
  • Top earners
  • Best sellers
  • Recommendations

I analysed a year of transactions in Python, from December 2010 to December 2011: 9,082 invoices, 3,059 customers and $362,902 in sales. I wrote up the results as a six-page sales report.

The report recommends focusing marketing and retention on the UK, planning stock around the top-earning products, testing promotions in slower months, and checking profitability before investing in smaller markets.

Built with Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Plotly, Streamlit and Jupyter.

View on GitHub (opens in a new tab)Read the report (PDF) (opens in a new tab)

What it found

  • One market carries the business: the UK brings in over 80% of revenue, and no other country reaches 4%.
  • The best sellers aren’t the top earners. None of the five most-bought products are among the five that bring in the most revenue.
  • Sales build from September and peak in November 2011 at over $50,000, more than twice a typical month earlier in the year.
  • The December 2011 drop isn’t real: the data stops after nine days, so I flagged it rather than report a fall in sales.

More projects

  • Bike Share Analysis

    When does a bike-share business make its money, and who are its riders?

    I used SQL to aggregate two years of ride data, 2021 and 2022, and built a Power BI dashboard of riders, revenue and profit by month, season and rider type.

    • Registered members make up 81% of riders, so keeping members matters more than chasing one-off casual riders.
    • Riders and revenue climb through spring, peak from June to September and fall away in winter, so bikes and staff can be planned around the season.

    Built with SQL (CTEs and aggregations), Power BI, Power Query and DAX.

  • World Cup Historical Overview

    What do 92 years of World Cup results say about who wins and how scoring has changed?

    A one-page Power BI dashboard covering every tournament from 1930 to 2022, the kind of quick reference a sports media team needs before a tournament.

    • Only 8 countries have won in 22 tournaments, and Brazil leads with 5 titles.
    • Total goals per tournament rose from 70 in 1930 to a record 172 in 2022 as the tournament expanded.

    Built with Power BI, Power Query, DAX and data modeling.

  • Coffee Sales Dashboard

    Which markets and customers drive a coffee seller’s sales?

    An interactive Excel dashboard that lets a manager filter sales by date, roast type, pack size and loyalty card, without writing a formula.

    • The US brings in about 79% of sales, $35,639 of $45,135, with Ireland and the UK far behind.
    • Even the top customer spent only $317, under 1% of sales, so revenue depends on many small buyers rather than a few big accounts.

    Built with Excel pivot tables, pivot charts, slicers and a timeline.
